# Timing test cartridge

Static test pages to photograph on a real Vectrex, to compare the emulator's analog timing (line
ends, corners) with the hardware. Button 4 switches pages.

| Page | What |
|---|---|
| SQUARES 08-17 | 16 squares drawn with the BIOS line routine (Draw_VLp, as Mine Storm uses), at scales (timer 1 lengths) $08..$17, labelled |
| LINES + END DOTS 08-17 | 16 horizontal lines at those scales; after each, a dot placed where the line should end (a beam move of the same vector, same scale): the gap between the line's lit end and the dot = its dark end |
| BOWTIES 08-17 | a bow-tie outline at those scales (one closed path) |
| SHIPS 0A-0D 5F / 7F | Mine Storm's ship (its own vector list, as Mine Storm draws it with Draw_VLp) at scales $0A-$0D; top row at Mine Storm's intensity $5F, bottom row $7F. Mine Storm draws it at $0C |
| BIG SHIPS 0A-0D 5F / 7F | the same with every vector doubled: twice the beam speed, the same timing, so the gaps are twice as wide (easier to photograph) |

Files: `timing_test.asm` (source, lwasm), `timing_test.bin` (32 KB), `timing_test_64k.bin` (the 32 KB
image twice, for a W27C512 on a PB6-switched cart: it boots in either bank). `roms\Timing Test.bin` is
the same for the emulator.

Photos: one per page, the whole screen in the frame, camera straight on, exposure short enough that
the lines are not blown out (the dots and gaps must stay visible). A close-up of each page's lower
half helps too.

Build: `lwasm --format=raw -o timing_test_raw.bin timing_test.asm`, then pad to 32768 bytes with $FF.

## Open bus test (`openbus_test.bin`)

One static page, **OPEN BUS READS**: each line reads an address no chip drives ($8000-$BFFF, the
$C000-$C7FF hole) with a different instruction and shows the byte in hex. On real hardware the
floating data bus decides the value; Mine Storm's split-mine bug (an object of type 4 gets a stray
vector list address, $3408) draws such bytes, so the emulator needs the real rule. The last line reads
$3408 inside this cartridge (the image is padded with $FF): how the cart maps its image there.

| Line | Read | Tells |
|---|---|---|
| LDA 9000 / 9055 / 90AA | extended | fixed value, or the last byte fetched ($00 / $55 / $AA)? |
| LDA C123 | extended, $C000-$C7FF | the other hole |
| LDA ,X 9000 | indexed (an idle cycle at $FFFF first) | does the idle cycle's BIOS byte ($00) show? |
| LDD 1,X HI / LO, LDB ,X+ | Draw_VLp's own reads | what the stray list really reads |
| LDA ,X C2E8 | where the emulated stray list walked | |
| LDA <00 DP90 | direct page | |
| LDA 3408 CART | inside this cartridge | the cart's padding / mirroring |

The emulator (2026-10-14): FF FF FF 00 FF FF FF FF 00 FF FF. Files: `openbus_test.asm`,
`openbus_test.bin` (32 KB), `openbus_test_64k.bin` (twice, for the 64 KB EPROM), `roms\Open Bus Test.bin`.

## CPU timing test (`cputime_test.bin`)

One static page, measured once at power-on, shown as 4 rows of 8 hex values (no labels: few
characters keep the BIOS text at 50 Hz). Generated by `make_cputime_test.py` (delays built
cycle-exact from NOP = 2 and BRN = 3).

Rows A and B: cycles of 6809 instructions, timed with timer 2 (a routine with the instruction minus an
empty one). Datasheet = emulator = the owner's Vectrex (first photo, 2026-10-14):

| A | NOP 02 | MUL 0B | PSHS+PULS 10 bytes 1E | LDA ,X 04 | LDA 1,X 05 | LDA ,X+ 06 | LDA [,X] 07 | LDA D,X 08 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **B** | LDA A,X 05 | LBRA 05 | CLRA+LBEQ taken 08 | LDA #1+LBEQ not taken 07 | JSR+RTS 0D | EXG 08 | TFR 06 | LEAX 1,X 05 |

Rows C and D: raw VIA readings; `off` = cycles from the end of the starting write to the read's last
cycle. Emulator values in brackets:

| C | T1 counter ($40) right after start [3C] | 2 cycles later [3A] | T1 ($04) after running out [EC] | T1 ($10) flag off 16 [00] | off 17 [00] | off 18 [40] | off 19 [40] | PB7 (T1 = the ramp switch) off 17 [00] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **D** | PB7 off 18 [80] | PB7 off 19 [80] | shift register flag after writing $FF, off 13 [00] | off 14 [00] | off 15 [04] | off 16 [04] | off 17 [04] | off 18 [04] |

The owner's Vectrex (2026-10-14): A and B as above; C 3C 3A **04** 00 00 40 40 00; D 80 80 00 00 **00 00**
04 04. The emulator then had EC (timer 1 kept counting down after running out; the 6522 reloads it from
the latch) and the shift register flag 2 cycles early: both fixed, the page now reads the same.

Files: `cputime_test.asm`, `cputime_test.bin` (32 KB), `cputime_test_64k.bin`, `roms\CPU Timing Test.bin`.

## Sample-and-hold test (`sh_test.bin`)

One page, generated by `make_sh_test.py`: the Y sample-and-hold's settling time. The DAC reaches the Y
integrator through a hold capacitor, connected while the multiplexer is on. Each dot: the hold is first
charged to a far value P (40 cycles), then set to the target T with the multiplexer on for only *d*
cycles (the number under the column: 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 12, 16, 24, 40), then the beam moves out and a
dot is drawn. Top row: P = -$7F, T = +$40; bottom row: P = +$7F, T = -$40. The short dim marks at both
ends of each row are the target height, drawn by the BIOS. A dot that falls short of the marks
(back towards P) did not settle in *d* cycles; how far short per *d* gives the hold's time constant. The emulator's hold is instant: all dots on the marks.

The owner's Vectrex (2026-10-14, 6 photos): only the d = 4 dot falls short, by ~1.5 DAC steps of the
191-step jump in both rows (0.8 %); from d = 6 the dots sit with the rest (the rows bow slightly toward
the screen's middle: the tube's geometry). The emulator now charges the hold 70 % of the gap per cycle
(VT_YSHK): d = 4 is 2 steps short, d >= 6 settled.

Files: `sh_test.asm`, `sh_test.bin` (32 KB), `sh_test_64k.bin`, `roms\S+H Test.bin`.

## Brightness test (`bright_test.bin`)

One page, generated by `make_bright_test.py`: 16 columns at intensity $02, $04, $08, $0C, $10, $18, $20,
$28, $30, $38, $40, $50, $60, $70, $78, $7F (dense at the low end, where the beam nears cutoff). Each
column: a vertical bar drawn with the BIOS line routine (the same length and speed for all), below it a
dot (the same resting time for all); the intensity in hex in two staggered rows at the bottom.
Photographed with a fixed exposure (manual: one setting for the whole series, the $7F bar not clipped),
the bars' and dots' brightness per intensity calibrates the emulator's brightness curve
(`vecrender.beam_z`): where the beam disappears, how fast it grows, where it saturates.

The owner's Vectrex (2026-10-14): counted by eye, 10 bars (the $20 one very faint) and 10 clear dots
(from $20); the photo (low exposure) peaks of the bars $28 / $30 / $38 / $40: 8 / 67 / 117 / 182 (from
$50 the camera clips). The emulator's curve below intensity 63 now has that cutoff (vecrender.beam_z /
beam_zdot) and shows the same 10 bars and 10 dots.

Files: `bright_test.asm`, `bright_test.bin` (32 KB), `bright_test_64k.bin`, `roms\Brightness Test.bin`.

## Wobble test (`wobble_50.bin`, `wobble_48.bin`, `wobble_25.bin`)

A still picture (two boxes and two lines of text) at a fixed frame rate, for the README's demonstration of
the mains hum wobble: the BIOS starts a frame every `Vec_Rfrsh` cycles, set here to 30,000 (50 fps, in
step with 50 Hz mains), 31,250 (48 fps: a game slightly over its budget - the picture sways) or 60,000
(25 fps: in step again). Built from `wobble_test.asm` with `-DREFRESH=n`, padded to 32 KB. (At 25 fps the
emulator's frame counter shows 50: it splits frames longer than 33,000 cycles in two.)

## FPS test (`fps_test.bin`, `fps_line.bin`)

A still picture whose frame rate changes every 128 frames, its name on screen: **50 FPS**, **40 FPS**, **30 FPS**,
**25 FPS**, **50/25 ALTERNATE**, **IRREGULAR** (frames of 20-43 ms), then again. On a real Vectrex the picture
keeps its brightness in every phase - only the mains wobble grows as the frame rate drops. `fps_test.bin` draws a
ring of boxes; `fps_line.bin` (built with `-DLINE`) one long horizontal line across the middle, to check a single
vector at every screen refresh. In the emulator: `roms\FPS Test.bin`, `roms\FPS Line Test.bin`.

Build: `lwasm --format=raw -o fps_test_raw.bin fps_test.asm` (add `-DLINE` for the line build), padded to 32 KB.

## Corner test (`corner_test.bin`)

One static page: the light of a beam that stops for a moment with the beam on - the corner dots games
show where one vector ends and the next starts (Vectorblade: rests of 8-31 cycles, intensities 47-95).
Drawn cycle-exact through the VIA (RAMP and /BLANK by hand, not the BIOS line routine); generated by
`make_corner_test.py`. Each sample: a line piece at speed $50 (like Vectorblade's lines), a rest of N
cycles with the beam on, a second piece. The columns: N = 0, 5, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96 (labelled
at the top; RAMP off to RAMP on).

| Rows (top to bottom) | What |
|---|---|
| 1-4 | straight, intensity $7F / $5F / $3F / $2F: the second piece continues the first, so the only difference between the columns is the rest |
| 5-6 | a 90 degree corner, $7F / $5F: the X speed flips at the rest, the Y speed stays (as in a game's shapes) |
| 7 | a dot, $7F: the beam on for N cycles without moving (the 5 column is 6 cycles) |

Every sample also has a short rest at its start and end (the beam switching on / off around RAMP): the
same in every column. The page takes at most ~28,000 cycles: a full 50 Hz refresh.

Photos (iPhone ProRAW, the **main** camera - the ultra-wide compresses its highlights): the whole page,
straight on, a fixed exposure that clips nothing (check the 96 column), 1/25 s or longer; a second one a
few stops brighter for the faint rows. Close-ups of the dots help to see their size.

Files: `corner_test.asm`, `corner_test.bin` (32 KB), `corner_test_64k.bin` (twice, for the 64 KB EPROM).

## Lag test (`lag_test.bin`)

One static page: how the spot follows the integrators (the deflection's lag, and the integrators' start and
stop delays). Generated by `make_lag_test.py`; every RAMP and /BLANK write is scheduled to the cycle (checked by
counting the instructions' cycles). Every sample: a horizontal line at speed $28, intensity $7F, drawn after 40
cycles at rest (the spot settled).

| Row | What | Columns (left to right) |
|---|---|---|
| A, START | RAMP on at 0, the beam on at k, RAMP off at 48, the beam off at 54: the lit line starts where the spot is at k | k = -4, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32, 40 (labelled) |
| B, END | the beam on at -4, RAMP on at 0, off at 48, the beam off at 48 + k: the lit line ends where the spot is at 48 + k | k = 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32, 40, 64 (labelled) |
| C, SCALE | the beam on at -4, RAMP on at 0, off at T, the beam off at T + 64: the whole path, a dot at both ends | T = 8, 16, 24, 32, 40, 48, 56, 64 (not labelled: time) |

Row C's length per cycle gives the photo's scale; rows A and B then give the spot's position against time at the
start and at the end of a line. The page takes at most ~28,600 cycles: a full 50 Hz refresh.

Photos (iPhone ProRAW, the **main** camera): the whole page, straight on, 1/25 s or longer, nothing clipped (the
row C dots are the brightest); sharp focus - the line ends are what is measured. A close-up of rows A and B helps.

Files: `lag_test.asm`, `lag_test.bin` (32 KB), `lag_test_64k.bin` (twice, for the 64 KB EPROM).

## Axis test (`axis_test.bin`)

One static page: corners in X, in Y and in both, against the beam's rest at the corner (the corner test only
flips X). Generated by `make_axis_test.py`, drawn cycle-exact through the VIA. Every sample: two 16-cycle line
pieces at speed $50, intensity $7F, a rest of N cycles between them (RAMP off to RAMP on, the beam on). A Y corner
needs the integrators stopped (the Y speed is loaded into its hold through the multiplexer, from the DAC - which
is the X speed too), so it is turned in one 16-bit store (RAMP off, the multiplexer on, the DAC = the new Y speed)
and restarted in another (RAMP on, the multiplexer off, the DAC = the X speed).

| Row | What | Rests N (labelled) |
|---|---|---|
| 1, X CORNERS | the X speed flips (-$50 -> +$50), the Y speed stays: "<" (as the corner test) | 0, 5, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48 |
| 2, Y CORNERS | the Y speed flips (-$50 -> +$50), the X speed stays: "v" | 5, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64 |
| 3, L CORNERS | X +$50 -> 0 and Y 0 -> -$50 at once: an L | 5, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64 |

In row 3 the DAC holds the Y speed during the rest, so right after the restart the X integrator sees it until the
X path has caught up: a small hook at the start of the vertical piece. Its size and shape show the X path's delay
and smoothing. The page takes at most ~28,200 cycles: a full 50 Hz refresh.

Photos (iPhone ProRAW, the **main** camera): the whole page, straight on, 1/25 s or longer, nothing clipped, sharp
focus; a close-up of rows 2 and 3 helps.

Files: `axis_test.asm`, `axis_test.bin` (32 KB), `axis_test_64k.bin` (twice, for the 64 KB EPROM).

## Beam test (`beam_test.bin`)

Four pages about the beam's analog path, picked with buttons 1-4 on controller 1 (generated by
`make_beam_test.py`, the timed parts drawn through the VIA by hand). All pages run under 30,000 cycles (50 Hz).

- **1 ZERO OFFSET**: per integrator offset ($00 $10 $20 $30 $40 $50 $E0 $C0, in that order) ZERO for ~400
  cycles with the offset set, then a big dot (12, 16 ... 40 cycles: growing with the order), the offset back to 0
  (RAMP off) and a small dot (8 cycles). Where ZERO goes with an offset, and whether the beam stays when the
  offset is reset. Then two lines drawn by the offset alone (speeds 0, RAMP on ~96 cycles, offset $20 and $E0).
  A frame of +-$60 at scale $7F gives the scale.
- **2 ZEROING**: four lit ZERO sweeps (intensity $3F) from the frame's corners - the path and its speed. Then 10
  partial zeros from start points at x = -$70 (a small dot each), N = 4 6 8 10 12 16 20 24 32 48 cycles from the
  top, each followed by a 16-cycle dot where the beam got to.
- **3 / 4 DAC**: the same string at text widths 60-66 and 52 56 58 67 68 72 76, each row from zero: the slant per
  width (the DAC's bit errors; Malban's "PrintStr (3) - DAC").

Files: `beam_test.asm`, `beam_test.bin` (32 KB), `beam_test_64k.bin` (twice, for the 64 KB EPROM), `roms\Tests\Beam Test.bin`.

## Gap test (`gap_test.bin`)

The gaps between joined lines, drawn with the BIOS's Draw_VLp as Mine Storm draws its ship and mines (generated by
`make_gap_test.py`). Buttons 1-3 on controller 1 pick the page; all pages run under 30,000 cycles (50 Hz).

- **1 ROWS**: per scale 6-13 (one full period of Draw_VLp's 7-cycle wait loop) a horizontal row of 10 joined segments
  at speed $7F, labelled at its left. A gap's share of its segment is its length in cycles / the scale, whatever the
  speed.
- **2 COLUMNS**: the same, vertical (Y goes through its sample-and-hold).
- **3 SQUARES**: a closed square per scale 6-13, each side two segments: the gaps at corners and on a straight join.

Files: `gap_test.asm`, `gap_test.bin` (32 KB), `gap_test_64k.bin` (twice, for the 64 KB EPROM), `roms\Tests\Gap Test.bin`.

## DAC test (`dac_test.bin`)

How far the beam goes per cycle at each DAC value (`make_dac_test.py`): is the deflection linear in the DAC, and how
long are the fixed delays at a line's start and end? Buttons 1-2 on controller 1 pick the page; under 30,000 cycles.

- **Page 1, rows:** per DAC value D (32, 48, 64, 85, 96, 112, 127, printed at the left) two lines from the same start
  (placed by a move at scale $7F): a long one of P / D cycles and a short one of half that. P is the same for all, so
  with a linear DAC every long line ends on the vertical reference line (placed by a move, X = 60 at scale $7F).
  Long minus short: the speed at that DAC value (the fixed delays cancel); twice short minus long: the delays.
- **Page 2, columns:** the same, vertical (Y goes through its sample-and-hold).

Why: Clean Sweep's centre box - on the real Vectrex its walls meet the sides; in the emulator they overshoot ~5 %.

Files: `dac_test.asm`, `dac_test.bin` (32 KB), `dac_test_64k.bin` (twice, for the 64 KB EPROM), `roms\Tests\DAC Test.bin`.


## Zero test (`zero_test.bin`)

Two pages, generated by `make_zero_test.py` (button 1: page 1, button 2: page 2): the value primed into the
integrators' common zero reference (mux channel 1), as two programs do it. Each page: 12 of Last Human's calibration
squares (a square traced 10 times with only its horizontal edges lit, at Last Human's object scale $14), each drawn
right after its own zero with the value V under it: 00, 01, 02, 03, 04, 06, 08, 12, 16, 24, 32, 35 (decimal). With
the right V for the console the ten passes fall on one another (two crisp lines); the further off, the more they fan
out as the beam drifts.

| Page | Sequence |
|---|---|
| LAST HUMAN ZERO | Last Human's ZeroCal, cycle for cycle: the reference loaded with V, then a 4-cycle pulse of DAC -1 |
| UVMC2 SDK ZERO | the UVMC2 SDK's zero block (uvm2_draw_reset, timed as its bus capture): nearly the same writes |

Why: in the emulator Last Human's 6809 cartridge is sharp at ~03, but Last Human's UVMC2 edition (which sends the
same sequence) at ~32, because the emulator gives UVMC2 games a faster zero reference (VT_ZREFK unset: 0.597; 6809
cartridges get the unit's 0.38). The SDK's own default is 35 ($23); Last Human's 6809 cartridge was checked at 2-3
on a real Vectrex. The emulator, rendered: with 0.38 both pages are sharpest at 03, with 0.597 at 32-35. The photos
of a real Vectrex tell which model is right - the page is longer than a frame (about 32 Hz on the tube).

Photos: both pages, the whole screen, camera straight on, an exposure where the separate passes stay visible; a
close-up of the squares that look sharpest. Note the console (the owner's Vectrex or another).

Files: `zero_test.asm`, `zero_test.bin` (32 KB), `zero_test_64k.bin`, `roms\Tests\Zero Test.bin`.

## Settle test (`settle_test.bin`, `settle_test_64k.bin`; delivered as `Settle Test.bin`, 64 KB)

The beam switch against the spot (2026-11-21): the corners' roundness says the spot settles slowly after a stop, the
joins' dots and the font say the beam switches fast - the earlier carts cannot tell the two apart. Here a line is drawn
by hand and only the beam's switching time varies per row, so the lit line's end (or start) is where the spot was at
that moment. Generated by `make_settle_test.py` (every VIA write on its cycle); buttons 1-4 pick the page; 50 Hz.
Picture: `settle_test_pages.png`.

| Page | What | Rows |
|---|---|---|
| 1 OFF | the beam on 12 cycles before RAMP on (a dot: the start), RAMP on L cycles, the beam OFF K cycles after RAMP off. Left: speed $50, L 40; right: $7F, L 24 | K = -12, -8, -5, 5, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32, 48 (top to bottom) |
| 2 ON | the beam ON K cycles after RAMP on, off 40 cycles after RAMP off (a dot: the end). Left: $50, L 48; right: $7F, L 40 | K = -12, -8, -4, 5, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32 |
| 3 PULSE | RAMP on throughout. Row 1: lit N cycles, dark 16 between; row 2: dark N cycles in a lit line (speed $40). Rows 3-4 the same at $7F | N = 4, 6, 7, 8, 12, 16 ($40); 4, 8, 16 ($7F) |
